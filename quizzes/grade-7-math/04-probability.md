## Instructions

Write probabilities as **fractions in simplest form** unless the question asks for a
decimal or percent. Remember:

> P(event) = (number of favorable outcomes) ÷ (total number of possible outcomes)

Every probability falls between **0** (impossible) and **1** (certain).

---

## Questions

**1.** A spinner has 8 equal sections and 3 of them are red. Find `P(red)`.

_______________________________

**2.** You roll one fair number cube (faces 1–6). Find `P(number greater than 4)`.

_______________________________

**3.** An event has a probability of 0.05. Describe in words how likely it is, and
explain what a probability of 1 would mean.

_______________________________

**4.** A bag holds 5 red marbles, 3 blue marbles, and 2 green marbles.
**(a)** Find `P(blue)`.
**(b)** Find `P(not green)`.

_______________________________

**5.** In an experiment, a coin is flipped 60 times and lands on heads 27 times.
What is the **experimental probability** of heads?

_______________________________

**6.** Using the spinner from question 1, about how many times would you **expect** to
land on red in 200 spins?

_______________________________

**7.** You flip a coin **and** roll a number cube.
**(a)** How many outcomes are in the sample space?
**(b)** Find `P(heads and a 3)`.

_______________________________

**8.** You flip two fair coins. Find `P(both heads)`.

_______________________________

**9.** A forecast says the probability of rain is 0.35. What is the probability that
it does **not** rain?

_______________________________

**10.** A spinner has 3 equal sections labeled A, B, and C. If you spin it twice, how
many outcomes are in the sample space?

_______________________________

**11.** Explain the difference between **theoretical** and **experimental**
probability.

_______________________________

**12.** A drawer has 4 black socks and 6 white socks. You draw one sock, **keep it**,
then draw another. If the first sock is black, what is `P(second sock is black)`?

_______________________________

## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Reasoning |
|---|--------|-----------|
| 1 | **3/8** | 3 favorable sections out of 8 equal sections |
| 2 | **1/3** | Only 5 and 6 work: 2/6 = 1/3 |
| 3 | **Unlikely** (it happens about 5 times in 100). A probability of **1** means the event is **certain** to happen | Probabilities near 0 are unlikely; near 1 are likely |
| 4 | (a) **3/10**  (b) **4/5** | Total = 10 marbles. Not green = 10 − 2 = 8, so 8/10 = 4/5 |
| 5 | **27/60 = 9/20 = 0.45** | Experimental probability uses actual results |
| 6 | About **75 spins** | 3/8 × 200 = 75 |
| 7 | (a) **12 outcomes**  (b) **1/12** | 2 coin results × 6 cube results = 12; exactly one outcome is (heads, 3) |
| 8 | **1/4** | Sample space is HH, HT, TH, TT — one of four is HH |
| 9 | **0.65** | Complement rule: 1 − 0.35 = 0.65 |
| 10 | **9 outcomes** | 3 × 3 = 9 (AA, AB, AC, BA, BB, BC, CA, CB, CC) |
| 11 | **Theoretical** probability is what *should* happen based on equally likely outcomes; **experimental** probability is what *did* happen based on trial results. The more trials you run, the closer experimental probability usually gets to theoretical | — |
| 12 | **3/9 = 1/3** | The first black sock is not replaced, so 3 black socks remain out of 9 total |
