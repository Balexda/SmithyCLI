## Instructions

A relationship is **proportional** when every pair of values has the same ratio
`y ÷ x`. That constant ratio is the **constant of proportionality**, `k`, and the
relationship can always be written as:

> **y = kx**

On a graph, a proportional relationship is a **straight line that passes through the
origin `(0, 0)`**.

## Answer Key

| # | Answer | Reasoning |
|---|--------|-----------|
| 1 | `k = 5`, so **y = 5x** | 10 ÷ 2 = 20 ÷ 4 = 30 ÷ 6 = 5 |
| 2 | **No** | The graph crosses the y-axis at 2, not at the origin. Check a pair: 5 ÷ 1 = 5 but 8 ÷ 2 = 4, so the ratio is not constant |
| 3 | **2.5** | 10 ÷ 4 = 2.5 |
| 4 | **$1.50** per notebook; **y = 1.5x** | 10.50 ÷ 7 = 1.50 |
| 5 | **y = 30** | 2.5 × 12 = 30 |
| 6 | **Table A** | In A, every ratio y ÷ x is 4. In B the ratios are 5, 4.5, and about 4.33 — not constant (B is y = 4x + 1) |
| 7 | (a) **65 mph** (b) **y = 65x** (c) **455 miles** | 195 ÷ 3 = 65; 65 × 7 = 455 |
| 8 | It gives the **constant of proportionality / unit rate** — the amount of y for exactly 1 unit of x | Substituting x = 1 into y = kx gives y = k |
| 9 | **9 pages per minute** — the unit rate, or constant of proportionality | The coefficient of x in y = kx is always the unit rate |
| 10 | The ratios are not constant: 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5, 11 ÷ 4 = 2.75, 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5 | y increases by a steady 2 per unit of x, but the line starts at 3 instead of 0 (y = 2x + 3), so it is linear but **not** proportional |
| 11 | **14** | 6 × 3 = 18, then 18 − 4 = 14 |
| 12 | **8** | Work backward: 20 + 4 = 24, then 24 ÷ 3 = 8 |
